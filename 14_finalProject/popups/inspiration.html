INSPIRATION
In the days leading up to leaving for Harvard, I spent some time browsing through past final projects, trying to come up with an idea that would be both interesting and feasible. Through that I came up with the idea of a Lidar Scanner My inspiration was really twofold. First, I thought about how this kind of technology could eventually help my mom with scanning buildings and turning that data into useful information for designing highly insulated panels The second inspiration came from my fascination with space exploration. This technology farther down the line, could in theory act as a system to be used to scan an area for potential dangers before humans enter it, and most of space being a vacuum light and thus LiDAR comes in handy. At first, I even wanted to build my own sensor using a simple Time-of-Flight concept to generate points. But that quickly proved unfeasible with the microcontrollers I had, since doing that properly requires an internal clock on the order of picoseconds, which was beyond what I could achieve with the hardware. So, I switched to using a ready-made LiDAR sensor to make the project practical, and the housing that would allow it to spin in 3D.
