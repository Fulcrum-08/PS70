By this point, I had created my MVP. Although it wasn’t fully functional,it was working in 2D and the design was mostly finalized. The biggest challenge was getting the scanner to power on. Initially, I had chosen a cheaper scanner that with a google search seemed to have no documentation and had possibly been discontinued.I then pivoted to a TF-Luna LiDAR scanner, which worked perfectly (highly recommend). Other issues included securing the LiDAR scanner inside its cylindrical casing and fitting the top stepper motor’s shaft into the shaft of the inner casing;I had made it a bit too small. 2025-08-7

I haven’t finished my project yet, and moving from the MVP to the final design ended up being more challenging than I initially expected. The biggest issue with the MVP was mostly the wiring, it got messy and complicated pretty quickly. To solve this, I used a slip ring and 3D-printed a central console that not only holds the slip ring shaft but also has grooves to neatly route the wires, which helped keep everything organized. On top of that, I added a third dimension to the scanning process. I programmed the stepper motors so that the bottom one rotates by one step (1.8 degrees) while the top one rotates 100 steps (about 180 degrees) before resetting and starting again. Meanwhile, the LiDAR sensor takes measurements continuously. In the end, I was able to scan a five-gallon bucket fairly accurately—it was the only enclosed space I had available at the home.
